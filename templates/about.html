ABOUT AQUAPULSE

River Water Quality Monitoring and Dissolved Oxygen Forecasting

AquaPulse is a machine-learning-based river water-quality monitoring and forecasting system developed to analyse dissolved oxygen conditions in the River Derwent.

The system brings together live river sensor measurements, live weather information, machine-learning predictions, anomaly detection and explainable AI in a single web application.

The main purpose of AquaPulse is to monitor current river conditions while also providing an indication of how dissolved oxygen levels may change over the next two hours.

LIVE RIVER MONITORING

AquaPulse monitors live AquaSensor measurements from three monitoring stations located along the River Derwent.

Live measurements

The application retrieves the latest available AquaSensor measurements and presents:

  • Dissolved Oxygen (mg/L) – the concentration of dissolved oxygen present in the river water.
  • Dissolved Oxygen (%) – dissolved oxygen expressed as a percentage.
  • Water Temperature (°C) – the current temperature of the river water.
WEATHER DATA

Open-Meteo live weather information

Alongside AquaSensor measurements, AquaPulse uses Open-Meteo weather data to provide additional environmental information for dissolved oxygen prediction.

Air Temperature
Sunshine / Solar Radiation
Cloud Cover

Combining river sensor measurements with weather information allows the prediction models to consider both current water conditions and surrounding environmental conditions when forecasting dissolved oxygen.

MACHINE-LEARNING FORECASTING

Predicting dissolved oxygen up to two hours ahead

One of the main features of AquaPulse is its ability to forecast future dissolved oxygen concentration in mg/L.

Three machine-learning approaches were evaluated as part of the project:

Linear Regression
Random Forest
XGBoost

The models were trained using historical river and environmental data, and their prediction performance was evaluated to determine the most suitable model for each forecasting task.

Forecast horizons

15 min 30 min 45 min 60 min 75 min 90 min 105 min 120 min

This allows AquaPulse to display the current measured dissolved oxygen value and provide short-term predictions extending up to two hours into the future.

ANOMALY DETECTION

Green, Orange and Red river alerts

AquaPulse includes an anomaly-detection and alert system designed to make unusual or potentially concerning river conditions easier to identify.

Green – Normal

Current and predicted river conditions are within the configured acceptable range and no significant anomaly has been detected.

Orange – Warning

A potentially concerning change or unusual trend has been identified and the station may require closer monitoring.

Red – Alert

A significant low dissolved oxygen or abnormal condition has been detected and requires attention.

EXPLAINABLE AI

Understanding model predictions with SHAP

AquaPulse incorporates SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) to support explainable machine learning.

Rather than presenting a prediction without explanation, SHAP can be used to examine how different input features contributed to a model prediction.

This provides greater transparency around model behaviour and helps explain the factors influencing dissolved oxygen forecasts.

PROJECT PURPOSE

Supporting clearer understanding of river data

AquaPulse was developed as an academic research project exploring how machine learning can support river water-quality monitoring.

The project demonstrates how historical and live environmental data can be transformed into useful predictive information and presented through an accessible web application.

By combining real-time measurements with short-term forecasting, AquaPulse demonstrates a possible approach for supporting earlier awareness of changes in dissolved oxygen conditions rather than relying only on observations of the current state.

i

Important research note

AquaPulse is an academic research prototype and is intended for research, analysis and demonstration purposes.

Machine-learning predictions and anomaly alerts should not be treated as official environmental warnings or used as a replacement for professional environmental monitoring and decision-making.

Data availability and forecasts also depend on the availability and quality of the underlying AquaSensor and Open-Meteo data.
